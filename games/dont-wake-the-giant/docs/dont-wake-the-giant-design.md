# Tiptoe Thieves

The user approved the giant-cottage concept on 2026-09-06 and asked to build it in Jumbleyard. The fourth game lives at `/dont-wake-the-giant`, with its own simulation, renderer, API and `giant:` room namespace. It extends the collection's rounded toy characters, cream controls, Fredoka headings and muted green/mustard palette into a lamplit cottage.

One sleeping pose supports two clearly warned reactions: an arm shift and a sneeze. Breathing and the arm are moving platforms. Rendering and collision use the same platform poses; grounded players and resting items inherit platform displacement, including players standing on pillows on moving surfaces. Bounded simulation substeps and serialized world state keep these rules shared by solo practice and authoritative multiplayer. The open chandelier is an arcade catch platform: launches pass up through its frame and descending players catch it.

Two to four friends share an eight-minute heist; solo practice uses the same rules. Collect treasure, carry one object at a time, place pillows to soften landings, use a movable teaspoon as a bridge, tickle the foot to provoke a sneeze, and help stunned friends. Crouching slows movement and reduces noise. Landings and metal impacts visibly increase a shared wakefulness meter. Thresholds warn before an arm shift or sneeze; full wakefulness or sunrise warns before a 25-second escape. Treasure banked outside remains secured even if a thief is caught. Players can leave early at the door. The team aims to secure 120 gold.

Keyboard and touch controls, camera switching, room invites, reconnect handling, restart and optional existing voice chat are included. Procedural sound works without paid generation or microphone access. Game noise is caused by actions, never microphone volume.

Verification covers moving-platform support across serialization, route traversal, pillow cushioning, reaction warnings and launches, treasure ownership/banking, escape deadlines, host-only starts, concurrent capacity and grabs, credential privacy, idempotency, stale input, departure recovery and voice isolation. Run the complete regression suite, type checking, build and a four-client HTTP integration. Browser interaction testing and human internet playtesting remain separate checks.

The user's visual revision enlarges the giant's body by about 20% in width and gives him a connected, rounded silhouette, separate legs and five-toed feet, fingered hands, shirt collar, expressive eyes, nose, moustache and beard. The larger bed keeps the existing climbing route. Waking opens his eyes, raises his upper body into a seated pose over 2.6 seconds, and adds reaching and looking gestures during the escape. The rotation and collision bounds derive from the same shared deadline. Passengers and loose items on the torso, including placed tools, are released before it rises; held treasure stays with its carrier. The warning and 25-second escape remain unchanged.

Treasure grows from seven pickups to 22, adding cut gems, coin pouches and a 100-gold crown. Pickups span the floor, drawers, books, bed, knees, pockets, shoulders, head and chandelier. The 120-gold goal stays accessible, while additional high loot rewards risky climbs. Floor loot alone remains below the target. Small gold rings distinguish collectible treasure from scenery.

The mobile movement revision keeps the game in a fixed viewport while inside a room, removes the 620/640-pixel gameplay minimum height, and prevents page overscroll. Menus and portalled dialogs retain their normal scrolling. Larger thumb controls and a compact landscape HUD fit short screens and safe areas. Canvas focus does not scroll the page. The shared joystick uses the thumb's touch-down position as its neutral origin, a continuous dead zone, exclusive pointer ownership and capture, and immediate release. Its thumb graphic updates directly without React renders for each move. Browser toolbar height changes do not reset an active drag; orientation, width changes, lost capture, a dialog or app switching stop movement. Joystick gestures cannot bubble to camera handlers, and two thumbs can move and jump independently.
