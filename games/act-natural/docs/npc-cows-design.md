# Player-farmer NPC cow slots

The host may add one NPC, fill all remaining seats, and remove individual NPCs between rounds. The room retains four total seats and requires a human farmer. Farmer duty rotates only among humans; a solo human remains farmer. Real players replace NPC seats in a full lobby, never during a round. Switching to the computer-farmer mode removes NPC seats. NPCs have no authentication or voice membership and never inherit host control. The last human leaving clears the NPC roster. Legacy peer rooms retain their existing behavior; new HTTP-authoritative farms offer NPC controls.

The AI combines utility scoring and risk-weighted A*. Utility weighs objective progress, distance, recently observed threat, current-job commitment, remaining time and other NPCs' declared jobs. It chooses keys, power, ladder, delivery, escape, blending and retreat. Movement uses a cow-clearance navigation graph, bounded replanning, line-safe smoothing and stuck recovery. Controllers have independent seeded personalities, variable decision/action delays and intermittent grazing. Held ladders remain slow and visible; every action goes through the same authoritative validation and collision/shock logic as a human.

Decisions receive the cow's private snapshot and teammates' job intentions. Farmer observations require local line of sight, with short-lived memory; no future player input or hidden human inventory is used. Brains, routes, goals, seeds and NPC-to-cow identity mappings remain server-only. Simulation is deterministic across serialization and independent of rendering.

Design references: [Game AI Pro: An Introduction to Utility Theory](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter09_An_Introduction_to_Utility_Theory.pdf) describes scoring contextual choices. [Red Blob Games: A* and movement costs](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html) explains combining travel cost with a distance heuristic. The farm's perception, utility weights, personality, coordination and controllers are specific to this game.

Validation covers complete seeded rounds, both exit strategies, threat response, route clearance and recovery, finite bounded input, serialization, bot membership/security, concurrent capacity/idempotency, role rotation, lifecycle, existing farm tests, production build and HTTP integration. Synthetic trials establish correctness and expose repetitive behavior; they cannot establish that the NPCs are indistinguishable from real people.
