# Free-game selection pilot

Use this with 12–20 new friend groups recruited by the project owner. Keep the current Stack or Sink, Crane Clash and Court Clash shortlist provisional until the sessions are complete. Do not activate paid admission or promise a final free trio during the pilot.

## Session

Recruit groups of two to four who have not played these games before. Include both desktop and phone play and some mixed-device groups. Give each group a random ID; do not collect names, contact details or account credentials in the scorecard. Ask them to join through the normal invite flow without coaching. Record any explanation they needed before their first round.

Each group plays one round of Stack or Sink, Crane Clash and Court Clash, plus one comparison game. Rotate the comparison game between Bungee Doubles, Sample Stampede and Zorb Clash. Rotate the order of all four games between groups so the first and last positions do not favor one title. After four rounds, let the group choose one game to replay without prompting. Keep the session to roughly 30–40 minutes and stop if a technical failure makes continuing frustrating.

## Record per group

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Group ID and date | Anonymous ID; local date |
| Players and devices | Number of players; desktop, phone or mixed |
| Invite to first round | Successful without help, successful with help, or failed; elapsed time |
| Game order | Four game IDs in played order |
| Per-game first round | Completed, abandoned or blocked; reason; controls questions; reconnects/crashes |
| Per-game response | Each player rates control clarity and desire to replay from 1–5; facilitator records a concrete observation |
| Free replay choice | One game the group chose and why |
| Group return intent | Would they arrange another session with this group? Why? |
| Purchase reaction | After play, explain that three games would stay free and the rest would require an individual roughly $5 pass; record each player's reaction without asking them to pay |

Record actual game events separately from opinions. A round that loads but cannot be finished is a failure, even if players liked its idea. Do not turn a tester's stated purchase intent into a conversion forecast.

## Decision

Review first-round completion, unaided understanding, replay choice, reliability on phones and desktop, and whether the trio offers distinct experiences. Give first priority to games that reliably get a new group through a complete round. If a shortlisted game fails that bar, compare the alternatives with the same evidence and run a focused follow-up before replacing it. Document the chosen trio, the observed trade-offs, and any remaining device issues in the final report. Only then update the shared free-game catalog and prepare the inactive access gate for launch testing.
